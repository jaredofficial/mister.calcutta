My ancestors were rangrez — cloth dyers from Rajasthan. Men who took something ordinary and made it vivid.

I've spent over two decades doing the same thing with a city.

Born and raised in Calcutta, I founded Calcutta Walks in the early 2000s — one of India's first heritage walking tour companies. I then restored a crumbling 1920s North Calcutta mansion into Calcutta Bungalow, a boutique heritage property that has been described as a love letter to the city.

Today, through Mister Calcutta on Instagram, I tell the stories of this extraordinary city to over 3 million people every month — stories the history books missed, delivered from the streets where they actually happened.

Calcutta was once the second city of the British Empire. The birthplace of fingerprinting, shampoo, Asia's first newspaper, and India's first gramophone recording. A city where Jewish merchants, Armenian traders, Chinese craftsmen, and Sufi saints built something together that the world has largely forgotten.

My job is to make sure it isn't forgotten.
