Who am I ?

My life in chapters..

“ My ancestors made ordinary cloth vivid.
I do the same with a city. ”

I was born and raised in Calcutta and I have spent over two decades doing what most people only dream of: turning an obsession into a vocation, and a city into a story.

My ancestors were Rangrez, cloth dyers from Rajasthan who settled in Calcutta decades ago and made their living transforming the ordinary into something vivid. I inherited that instinct, just not the profession.

A graduate of St. Xavier's College, Kolkata, I broke away from my family's trade with a single conviction that Calcutta was the most cosmopolitan, misunderstood, and under-told city on earth and that someone needed to fix that. I decided that someone would be me.

Early 2000s

Calcutta Walks

Founded in 2007, Calcutta Walks was one of India's first heritage walking tour companies.

Not a tour company in the conventional sense, Calcutta Walks is dedicated to the notion of pro-Calcutta, eco-friendly tourism; walking the city rather than tooling around in gas-guzzling vehicles, led by people I call explorers, never guides.

Together, we have conducted over 10,000 walks, covered 110,000 kilometres on foot, and hosted over 50,000 walkers from across the world.

2013-2018

Calcutta Bungalow

For years, I was showing people beautiful, crumbling buildings, pointing at carved balconies and peeling facades, telling their stories, and then walking away.

At some point that started to feel insufficient.

In 2013, while leading one of my walks, I had my moment of inflection that words alone wouldn't save the city, I realised I needed to do something tangible.

I found a 1926 townhouse in the heart of North Calcutta, once the home of the Majumdar family, then abandoned for years. The walls needed more love than I initially anticipated but the bones were good, and there was something about the place that felt worth saving.

None of what followed was done alone. My closest friend and business partner Chris Chen came on board, designer Swarup Dutta brought the vision to life room by room, and a team of craftspeople did the kind of work that simply cannot be rushed.

It took two years to acquire and three years to restore the building to its original form and then in 2018, Calcutta Bungalow opened its doors to all.

2018

US State department IVLP Fellow

In 2018, I was selected as an International Visitor Leadership Program Fellow in Sustainable Tourism by the United States government, one of the most competitive cultural exchange fellowships awarded to emerging international leaders.

It was one of those moments that made me realise that what felt like a very personal, very Calcutta obsession was actually speaking to something universal that people everywhere are hungry for travel that means something, that connects rather than consumes. I came back from that fellowship more convinced than ever that the work mattered.

2020

Calcutta Unschoolers

During the lockdown, my wife Shaheera and I made a decision that raised a few eyebrows. We chose to unschool our twins; Isa and Shehrezad. No classrooms. No textbooks. No bells telling them when to stop thinking about one thing and start thinking about another.

Isa, our son, is obsessed with cricket. And so he taught himself mathematics through the game - scorecards, run rates, strike rates, averages, probability, statistics. Nobody sat him down and taught him any of it. The sport gave him numbers that actually meant something, attached to moments he actually cared about. That is what real learning looks like.

Shehrezad, our daughter, is the creative soul of the family. She reads everything she can get her hands on - fiction, history, philosophy, cookbooks. She attends workshops, makes things with her hands, draws, writes, asks questions that frankly leave us scrambling for answers.

Together, they learn by being in the world, meeting people from different walks of life, visiting places that have stories to tell, sitting with craftspeople and artists and farmers and historians and just absorbing. At 12, they have already visited 15 countries. But the travel is just one part of it. What we are really trying to do is raise humans who are curious, who are kind, who know how to learn anything because they have never been told that learning has a fixed shape.

What began as our family's private conviction has slowly grown into something larger. This is a community we are building for parents and children who are asking the same questions we asked and finding, as we did, that the answers are more liberating than frightening.

2024

Art Cafe

The Art Cafe was never supposed to be my project. It belonged to Pravesh and Yachna who had built something quietly wonderful in Kalimpong, a cafe rooted in the belief that a space can hold a community together. When the conversation began about bringing it to Calcutta, it felt natural. Because it was the same instinct as Calcutta Bungalow - find a forgotten space, restore it with love, fill it with the flavours and textures of the place it belongs to.

In 2024, Art Cafe became part of our family of brands. Shaheera, my wife and I came on board alongside Chris and Suman, and together with Pravesh and Yachna, brought it to Kolkata.

Two outlets in the heart of the city, each a partnership between old friends who happen to believe in the same things. Jhol momos, buckwheat crepes, flourless cakes, cheese sourced directly from the Kalimpong hills. And walls that have something to say.

2025

Kalimpong Bungalow

When it came to us, we did not want to change it. We wanted to honour it. We renamed it Kalimpong Bungalow, not to rebrand it, but to root it more honestly in the place it belongs to. Kalimpong has a colonial past where Scottish missionaries, Tibetan traders, and Nepali communities once lived side by side, and left traces that are still visible if you know where to look. This bungalow sits inside all of that history.

You may come for the mountains. You will stay for everything else.

2026

Calcutta Rooms

The same instinct that built Calcutta Bungalow - find a beautiful, forgotten space and give it a second life now extends across the city's neighbourhoods.

Calcutta Rooms is our version of a home stay, but rooted in the same philosophy of slow, honest hospitality. We begin with Sen's Homestay, a lovingly kept home in the heart of South Calcutta. It will not be the last. There are too many houses in this city with too many stories still left in their walls and we intend to find them.

Now

Mister Calcutta & Calcutta Creative Unit

For over 20 years, I could only reach the people who came to me, who walked through the door, booked a walk, stayed at the bungalow. And I was grateful for every single one of them. But there was a whole world of people who would never think to come to Calcutta, who had never heard its stories, who did not even know what they were missing. I wanted to reach them. So I started telling Calcutta's stories online.

Through @mister.calcutta on Instagram & YouTube, I make short films about the city's Armenian community, its last Jewish families, its Chinese neighbourhood, its forgotten graveyards, its disappearing crafts. About the fact that the world's first fingerprint bureau was here. That the cure for malaria was discovered here. Calcutta is a city of firsts that somehow never got around to telling its own story loudly enough. We are trying to fix that, one video at a time, from the exact streets and doorways where these stories unfolded.

Alongside this, my team and I are building the Calcutta Creative Unit - CCU. The name is not accidental. CCU are also the three letters that have always stood for this city. Born here, shaped by everything this city has taught us about depth, about patience, about telling things straight. The market is full of agencies that chase reach, manufacture content, and measure success in vanity metrics. What it lacks is meaningful storytelling, real brand building, and the patience to see it through. CCU exists to fill that gap. We work with brands built with intention, helping them uncover their real story and bring it to the world through content, campaigns, and creativity that actually means something. We don't just grow audiences. We build communities. Calcutta taught us that. Now we want to take that to the world.

International Collaborations

Every city in the world has a Calcutta thread connecting back to communities across the globe. These threads are real, documented, and waiting to be told. The Armenians of Calcutta trace back to Isfahan. The Baghdadi Jewish community connects to Baghdad and Tel Aviv. The Hakka Chinese of Tiretti Bazaar have cousins in Penang and Toronto. The indentured labourers who left from Calcutta's docks in the 19th century built communities in Mauritius, Fiji, Trinidad, and South Africa.

If you are a cultural institution, a diaspora organisation, a destination, or simply a place with a story that connects back to this city, I want to find that thread and tell it to the world. I can visit, explore, film, and create content that brings your story to a global audience. I can speak, walk, and collaborate. The format is always secondary. The story comes first.
